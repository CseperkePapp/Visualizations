Anatomy of a takedown

Everyone's investor, everyone's rival: how Anthropic ended up surrounded

In barely 72 hours this June, the U.S. government pulled Anthropic's most powerful AI model off the market. To see why, you have to trace a decade of money, compute, and grudges — and the four giants who are bankrolling Anthropic and competing with it at the same time.

June 16, 2026 · A reported timeline, with the documented facts and the open questions kept separate.

On June 9, Anthropic shipped Fable 5, the public version of its Mythos model and, by its own billing, the most capable AI it had ever released. Three days later it was gone. On June 12, the Commerce Department invoked national-security export controls and gave Anthropic roughly 90 minutes to disable Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — not just abroad, but for every foreign national, including its own employees. Unable to filter cleanly by nationality, Anthropic pulled both models worldwide.

The proximate trigger was a tip: Amazon CEO Andy Jassy told senior administration officials that his researchers had jailbroken Fable 5 to extract restricted cyberattack information. The strange part is that Amazon is Anthropic's single largest investor. To understand how a company's own backer ended up handing the government the lever that shut it down, you have to back up — about a decade.

What the relationship web makes obvious

Scrub to June 2026 and every arrow points inward. Anthropic isn't one player among equals — it's the hub everyone is wired into, which is exactly why a single government letter could ripple through the whole structure.

The contradictions are the point. Amazon, Google, and Microsoft each carry three relationships to Anthropic at once: they invest in it, supply its compute, and either compete with it or build products on it. These aren't allies or rivals; they're both. Amazon is the sharpest case — the only investor that also acted against it, the betrayal at the center of June's story. Google sits in a nearly identical position — investor, compute supplier, and rival through Gemini — but never turned. Same hand, different play.

And the dependency runs deep enough to be the real vulnerability. By mid-2026, Anthropic is renting compute from Musk's SpaceX, running on Amazon's infrastructure, and sitting on 5 gigawatts of Google TPUs — its three biggest lifelines being a former insulter, the company that just informed on it, and a direct rival. Every Big Tech "AI couple" has effectively gone polyamorous: Microsoft drifts from OpenAI toward Claude; OpenAI signs a $50B deal with Amazon; Google funds the lab its own Gemini is trying to beat.

What's documented, and what's inferred. The events above are sourced: the investments, the compute deals, the competition, Amazon's tip-off, and the government's actions all come from public reporting. What the reporting does not establish is motive. It's tempting to read the June takedown as payback for Anthropic's February refusal to drop its safety limits for the Pentagon — and the two threads may well reinforce each other — but no source proves the suspension was retaliation, or that Amazon's motive was anything beyond the stated security concern. The structure is real; the "it's all one war" reading is a hypothesis laid over it. The biggest genuine open question: why Amazon moved against a company it has invested billions in.
